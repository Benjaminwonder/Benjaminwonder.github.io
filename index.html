Semiclassical chaos–quantum hybrid random number generator

Chaotic double-pendulum trajectories modulate the Hamiltonian of a driven qubit. The response is extracted, conditioned, and statistically certified as a high-entropy bitstream.

Problem
Can a chaotic classical system serve as an entropy source for a quantum degree of freedom, with entropy claims that survive physically realistic dynamics and standard statistical assessment?
Approach
Coupled pendulum–qubit simulation in Python (NumPy, SciPy, QuTiP) under a Lindblad master equation; 4:1 SHA-256 conditioning; NIST SP 800-90B on the raw source and SP 800-22 on the conditioned stream; a physical pendulum for measured trajectories.
My role
Co-developed the generator; built the simulation and extraction pipeline; designed and built the instrumented pendulum; ran the entropy assessment. First author on the manuscript.

Key decisions

  1. Open-system model

    Replaced a closed-system Schrödinger model with a Lindblad master equation after the unitary model produced unphysically idealized dynamics, capturing realistic relaxation and dephasing.

  2. Conservative integration

    Moved the classical integrator to an implicit conservative scheme to eliminate unphysical energy growth: relative drift 1.7×10−6 over 2×104 s.

  3. From simulation to measurement

    Designed and built a physical double pendulum with two wireless rotary encoders, extending the work to measured trajectories and phase portraits.

  4. Conservative entropy claim

    Reported the minimum across the full SP 800-90B non-IID estimator suite on the raw source, and validated the conditioned stream separately against all 15 SP 800-22 categories.

Aerospace relevance Stiff, energy-sensitive integration and open-system noise models are the same numerical problems that govern hypersonic-flow and combustion-instability simulation. Certified entropy sources are a primitive for secure telemetry and command links.

Collective cell mechanics: an agent-based tissue model

Does tissue spread during axolotl epiboly as a Kelvin–Voigt viscoelastic solid? A mechanically extended Vicsek simulator, built and verified to answer it.

Test suite · pytest

Problem
Determine whether epiboly tissue spreading is consistent with Kelvin–Voigt viscoelastic-solid mechanics.
Approach
Vicsek alignment extended with a Hookean spring force, Voronoi-tessellated cell contacts, and inertial dynamics; Python/NumPy simulator delivered as a Streamlit app.
My role
Extended the model, derived the integrator stability bounds, and built the simulator and its validation suite.

Key decisions

  1. Mechanics in the model

    Added a Hookean spring force and Voronoi tessellation to the standard Vicsek model to define cell contacts and neighbor geometry.

  2. Inertial dynamics

    Random per-cell masses, headings driven by net force, and energy-conserving elastic collisions.

  3. Derived, not tuned, time-stepping

    Derived stability bounds that set adaptive symplectic sub-stepping, verified against the analytic two-body oscillator.

  4. Built to be shared

    Fault-isolated model lineages in a Streamlit app shared with a collaborator, so variants can evolve without breaking the validated baseline.

Aerospace relevance Particle-based many-body simulation verified against analytic limits: the same V&V discipline behind CFD, DSMC for rarefied hypersonic flow, and multi-agent GNC.
